Choosing Grace Over Every Grievance

"Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you." — Colossians 3:13

Relationships can be messy. People around us inevitably make mistakes, say the wrong thing, or test our patience. The apostle Paul reminds us that walking together in faith requires two ongoing habits: bearing with one another and forgiving freely. Bearing with someone means offering room for human weakness, while forgiving means choosing to release the offense when real hurt occurs.

The secret to extending this kind of grace does not come from pushing through on your own strength. It flows directly from remembering what Jesus has done for you. You are called to forgive not because the other person earned it, but because the Lord has already lavished His complete forgiveness on you. When you anchor your heart in God's mercy, releasing a grievance becomes an act of gratitude and freedom.

Living It Out

Think of one person who has recently annoyed you or caused you pain. Take a quiet moment today to name that grievance to God, thank Him for His mercy toward you, and make a conscious choice to release the offense. Then, reach out with a simple act of kindness or a friendly greeting to them.

Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank you for loving me and forgiving my many faults so completely. Soften my heart today whenever I feel tempted to hold a grudge against someone else. Give me the strength to extend the very same grace to others that you have so generously given to me. Amen.
